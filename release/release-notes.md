- Fixed: a correctly configured inbound surface rejected every message sent to it. A webhook POST that carried the right secret came back `401 Unauthorized`, on a surface whose settings screen showed it connected and whose secret was exactly what the sender was using. Nothing you could change fixed it, because nothing you had done was wrong.
- What went wrong: the platform runtime bundled with 1.22.0 had begun moving surface credentials out of plain settings and into the secret store, leaving a `goodvibes://secrets/...` reference behind in their place. Twelve channel adapters then compared that reference, character for character, against the secret the caller actually presented. The two are never equal, so the check could only ever fail.
- Telegram in webhook mode was the worst of it. The daemon registers the webhook with the real secret, so Telegram sends the real secret and the adapter rejects it against the reference — every update, from the moment the credential was moved. Telegram in polling mode was unaffected, which is why the surface could look healthy while nothing arrived.
- How it is fixed: every one of those checks now resolves the credential first, through one shared path with three honest answers — resolved, not configured, or configured but unreadable. A credential that cannot be read answers `503`, not `401`: nothing is wrong with the caller, and telling a sender it is unauthorized invites them to change a secret that was correct all along.
- If you turned a webhook off, changed a secret, or re-registered a surface while chasing this, none of that was needed. Put the secret back to what it was and the surface works.
- Updated the bundled GoodVibes platform runtime to 1.19.2, which carries this fix.
