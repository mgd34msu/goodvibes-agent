- **Fixed: a chat message gets an answer, never workflow paperwork.** A conversational message could be turned into a full write-review-fix-confirm chain by a keyword heuristic that matched wording like "review the route" inside the transcript, and the reply sent back was the chain's status line — "passed (review 10/10); commit skipped: not a git repository" — three turns in a row. The conversation gate's decision is now authoritative: conversation is answered, work is proposed and waits for agreement, and when a chain does legitimately run, its reply carries what the agent actually found or did. Duplicate replies from the two completion reporters are gone.
- **Added: personal information shared in conversation gets captured.** A conversational channel turn previously ran with no tools at all — it could talk and do nothing. It now carries the profile capture tool with per-run owner authority: a trip itinerary pasted into chat lands in the owner profile's Plans section with its dates, flights, travelers and confirmation number, and the reply states concretely what was stored. Configurable via `profile.conversationalCapture` (default on) and `profile.ownerChannels` (defaults to the occasions nudge channel).
- **Fixed: a chat session is filed as a chat.** Channel-originated sessions are classified by their channel instead of as project sessions rooted in a filesystem path, rollover re-derives the classification, and a sender on the channel's owner allowlist is attributed as the owner principal instead of unknown in his own private chat.
- **Changed: money settings hold the amount you would say out loud.** The payment budget keys drop their unit suffix and hold plain amounts in your currency, written exactly as you give them: 100 is a hundred dollars, 19.99 is nineteen ninety-nine, and $100, 100.00 and 100 all mean the same hundred — decimals allowed, never forced. Money detection is schema-typed, so the settings modal and search treat every money field correctly without name-sniffing.
- **Fixed: a settings file is rewritten only by its owner.** A newer client reading the daemon's settings migrates its in-memory view and leaves the file bytes untouched, so updating one product can no longer rewrite shared settings out from under an older running daemon. A migrated file names the reader that understands it, and an older reader refuses loudly instead of silently skipping your spending limits.
